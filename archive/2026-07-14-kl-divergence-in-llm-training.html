KL divergence is widely used in LLM training, here is a post attempting to revisit the intuition behind them and the differences between them.

KL divergence basics

First of all, some basics about KL divergence:

KL divergence is basically a “distance” metric to measure how different two probability distributions are.

Assuming we have P(x) as the true distribution and Q(x) is as the approximating probability distribution.

The formula of KL divergence looks like this:

\[D_{KL}(P || Q) = \Sigma_{x \in \mathbb{X}} \: P(x) \: log \frac{P(x)}{Q(x)}\]

A simple interpretation is to interpret it as the expected excess surprisal from using the approximation Q instead of P when the actual is P.

(Note that the formula itself is in the structure of the expectation formula.)

KL divergence is asymmetric, i.e. flipping the position of P(x) and Q(x) will change the behaviour. Hence, there are forward KL and reverse KL.

Forward KL:

(expectation over samples drawn from P)

\[D_{KL}(\textcolor{green}{P || Q}) = \Sigma_{x \in \mathbb{X}} \: \textcolor{red}{P(x)} \: log \textcolor{blue}{\frac{P(x)}{Q(x)}}\]

Reverse KL:

(expectation over samples drawn from Q)

\[D_{KL}(\textcolor{green}{Q || P}) = \Sigma_{x \in \mathbb{X}} \: \textcolor{red}{Q(x)} \: log \textcolor{blue}{\frac{Q(x)}{P(x)}}\]

tip: a quick way to identify whether it is taking expectation over samples drawn from P or Q is to look at the “non-fraction” term in the formula (highlighted in red in the formulas above)

Intuition to understand the difference

Forward KL

  • P(x) is large and Q(x) is small → log term would be large, the loss would be large: penalize the model for not assigning probability on the tokens that the true distribution place probability on → penalize the most when the model doesn’t assign probability on the correct tokens.

  • P(x) is small and Q(x) is large → log term would be small, loss is small → doesn’t penalize much when the model places probability on the tokens that the true distribution doesn’t place probability on → doesn’t penalize as much when the model assign probability on the wrong tokens.

This is why we say that forward KL is encouraging mode-covering behaviour → it tries to cover as much as possible to avoid penalty.

Reverse KL (the opposite of forward KL)

  • P(x) is large and Q(x) is small → loss is small → doesn’t penalize much when the model doesn’t assign probability on the correct tokens

  • P(x) is small and Q(x) is large, loss is large → penalize the most when the model assign probability on the wrong tokens.

This is why reverse KL encourages the mode-seeking behaviour → it avoids assigning probability on the wrong tokens, so it will stick to the tokens that it knows are correct.

KL divergence in LLM training

KL divergence is very widely used in LLM training.

An overview

Pre-training/SFT

The loss used in pre-training/SFT is the cross-entropy loss

\[\mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}} = H(P_{\text{data}}, Q_\theta)= H(P_{\text{data}}) + D_{KL}(P_{\text{data}} \parallel Q_\theta)\]

The ground truth used for each token is a one-hot vector (only one token has probability = 1 and all other tokens have probability = 0).

Since it is a one-hot vector, the cross-entropy loss equation collapses to just a negative log-likelihood equation:

\[\mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}} = -\mathbb{E}_{x \sim P_{\text{data}}} [\log Q_\theta(x)]\]

Add-on: difference between pre-training and SFT:

  • pre-training: all tokens are involved in training (loss are calculated for all tokens)

  • SFT: a loss mask is used. The tokens in the prompt are not involved in the training, only tokens in the response are calculated loss.

Knowledge distillation

\[\mathcal{L}_{\text{KD}} = \sum_{x} P_{\text{teacher}}(x) \log \left( \frac{P_{\text{teacher}}(x)}{Q_{\text{student}}(x)} \right)\]

Basically the forward KL formula.

RLHF (PPO)

PPO loss is made up of two parts:

  1. the reward component

  2. the KL divergence component

\[\mathcal{L}_{\text{PPO}} = -\mathbb{E}_{x \sim \mathcal{D}} \left[ \mathbb{E}_{y \sim \pi_\theta(\cdot|x)} \left[ r_\phi(x, y) - \beta \log \frac{\pi_\theta(y|x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y|x)} \right] \right]\]

This part is the reverse KL divergence

\[\mathbb{E}_{y \sim \pi_\theta} \left[ \log \frac{\pi_\theta(y|x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y|x)} \right] = D_{KL}(\pi_\theta \parallel \pi_{\text{ref}})\]

DPO

\[\mathcal{L}_{\text{DPO}} = -\mathbb{E}_{(x, y_w, y_l)} \left[ \log \sigma \left( \beta \log \frac{\pi_\theta(y_w|x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_w|x)} - \beta \log \frac{\pi_\theta(y_l|x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_l|x)} \right) \right]\]

On-policy distillation

Basically the reverse KL formula.

\[\mathcal{L}_{\text{On-Policy KD}} = \mathbb{E}_{x \sim Q_{\text{student}}} \left[ \log \frac{Q_{\text{student}}(x)}{P_{\text{teacher}}(x)} \right]\]
